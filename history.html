BEFANG VILLAGE.

Befang is a village located in the northwest
region of Cameroon, under Wum Subdivision.
it is one of the major Villagers of the Aghem tribe, which speaks the
Yemne-kugh language (often refered to simply as Aghem).
Origins and Settlement: the history of Befang,
like many Cameroonian villages, is deeply rooted
in oral tradition. it is believed that
the early inhabitants of Befang migrated from the Tikar region,
a common origin for many groups in the grassfields area of Cameroon. The founders
sought fertile land, security, and space to expand their families.
They eventually settled in the rich, forested area that is now Befang,
where agriculture and hunting flourished. Traditional Leadership: Befang is traditionally governed by
a Fon (chief), who plays a central role in the cultural, political,
and spiritual life of the people.The village is organized under a
chieftaincy system, and the Fon is supported by various notables
, elders,and secret societies that uphold customs and maintain order. During German colonization
in the late 19th
century, Bamenda was integrated
into German Kamerun.
After Germany's defeat in World
War I, the territory was
handed over to the British under
a League of Nations mandate.
Bamenda then became part of
British Southern Cameroons
and was administered
from Nigeria.
