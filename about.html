THE HISTORY OF CAMEROON

Before European colonization, it was inhabited by various ethnic group and powerful kingdoms like the Bamoun, Tikar and the Fulani.
Portiguese explorers arrived in the 15th century and named the region after the prawns in the Wouri River calling it "Rio dos Camaroes".

In 1884, Germany made it a colony known as "Kamerun". After Germany's defeat in world war 1, the territory was divided between France and Britain.
French Cameroon gained independence in 1960, becoming the Republic of Cameroon. In 1961, the southern part of British Cameroon joined the Republic of Cameroon, creating united country.

Since then Cameroon has remained a bilingual nation, with both French and English as official languages,
though it faces ongoing challenges, especially between the Anglophone and Francophone Regions.
